# Current-State Cyber Risk Assessment

## Assessment Purpose

This assessment establishes the baseline cybersecurity risk management environment for the simulated organization and identifies the governance, process, and reporting gaps that must be addressed to establish an enterprise cyber risk management program.

## Organizational Scenario

The organization has established cybersecurity functions including vulnerability management, cloud security, identity and access management, third-party risk management, compliance, and incident response.

These functions identify cybersecurity issues; however, there is no centralized process for consistently translating those findings into enterprise risks that are assessed, assigned to accountable owners, treated, monitored, and reported to leadership.

## Current-State Assessment

| Capability | Current State | Key Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Risk Governance | Risk decisions occur within individual teams | No centralized cyber risk governance model |
| Risk Policy | General security policies exist | No formal Cybersecurity Risk Management Policy |
| Risk Identification | Multiple security functions identify issues | Findings are not consistently converted into enterprise risks |
| Risk Intake | Email, tickets, spreadsheets, and meetings are used | No standardized risk intake process |
| Risk Taxonomy | Teams use different risk terminology | No enterprise cyber risk taxonomy |
| Risk Scoring | Severity is assessed differently across teams | No standardized likelihood and impact methodology |
| Risk Register | Risks are tracked across separate sources | No centralized enterprise cyber risk register |
| Risk Ownership | Ownership is determined informally | No standardized accountability model |
| Risk Appetite & Tolerance | Decisions are made case-by-case | No defined tolerance or escalation criteria |
| Risk Treatment | Remediation occurs within individual teams | Treatment decisions are not consistently documented |
| Risk Acceptance | Exceptions may receive management approval | No standardized acceptance authority or expiration requirements |
| Remediation Tracking | Tickets and spreadsheets are used | Limited visibility into overdue remediation |
| Continuous Monitoring | Teams monitor issues independently | No consolidated enterprise risk monitoring process |
| Executive Reporting | Security metrics are periodically reported | Limited enterprise-level cyber risk visibility |
| KPIs / KRIs | Operational metrics exist | No formal cyber risk KPI/KRI framework |

## Key Gaps Identified

The assessment identified the need to establish:

- Centralized cybersecurity risk governance
- Cybersecurity Risk Management Policy
- Defined risk ownership and accountability
- Enterprise cyber risk taxonomy
- Risk appetite and tolerance criteria
- Standardized likelihood and impact methodology
- Centralized enterprise cyber risk register
- Formal risk treatment and acceptance workflow
- Remediation monitoring and escalation
- Continuous risk monitoring
- Cybersecurity KPIs and KRIs
- Executive cyber risk reporting

## Target State

The target environment will establish a centralized cyber risk management capability that enables:

**Risk Sources → Standardized Intake → Risk Assessment → Centralized Risk Register → Risk Treatment → Remediation → Continuous Monitoring → Executive Reporting**

The program will provide defined governance, consistent risk assessment, accountable risk ownership, documented treatment decisions, escalation of risks outside established tolerance, and leadership visibility into enterprise cybersecurity risk.

## Recommended Implementation

### Phase 1 – Foundation & Governance
Establish governance, policy, ownership, risk appetite/tolerance, taxonomy, and assessment methodology.

### Phase 2 – Risk Assessment & Treatment
Implement risk intake, centralized risk tracking, inherent/residual risk assessment, control effectiveness, and treatment workflows.

### Phase 3 – Operationalization & Monitoring
Implement remediation tracking, escalation, recurring risk reviews, and continuous monitoring.

### Phase 4 – Risk Monitoring & Executive Reporting
Develop KPIs, KRIs, heat maps, risk aging analysis, and executive dashboards.

## Assessment Conclusion

The organization has established cybersecurity functions capable of identifying security issues but lacks a centralized governance structure and standardized process for managing those issues as enterprise cybersecurity risks.

The assessment therefore supports establishing an Enterprise Cybersecurity Risk Management Program that standardizes risk identification, assessment, ownership, treatment, monitoring, and executive reporting.
